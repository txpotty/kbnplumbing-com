    Frequently Asked Questions About Grease Trap Cleaning in Myrtle Beach

    Small indoor grease traps typically cost $150 to $400 per cleaning. Large outdoor interceptors often run $300 to $1,200 depending on size and buildup. Regular maintenance plans can lower the cost per visit.

    Grease trap cleaning removes fats, oils, and grease (FOG), along with food solids, sediment, and wastewater. This material is often referred to as 'brown grease' and requires specialized handling and disposal to avoid environmental contamination.

    Staff can sometimes clean very small under-sink traps, but waste must be disposed of properly. Outdoor interceptors require a vacuum truck and a licensed hauler, and many cities require professional records.

    The terms are often used interchangeably, but 'grease interceptor' usually refers to larger, outdoor units with capacities over 500 gallons, while 'grease trap' usually describes smaller, indoor units under 100 gallons, such as under-sink models. Both serve the same function of separating FOG from wastewater.

    FOG stands for Fats, Oils, and Grease. When poured down drains, FOG cools and solidifies, adhering to pipe walls and eventually leading to severe clogs. In grease traps, it separates from wastewater, but if not regularly removed, it overflows into the sewer system, causing blockages and environmental issues.

    Yes, we provide a service record after every cleaning. It includes the date, volume removed and condition of the trap, which you can show inspectors.

    KBN Plumbing services all common types of grease traps and interceptors, including passive hydromechanical grease interceptors (HGIs), automatic grease removal units (AGRUs), and large gravity grease interceptors (GGIs). We handle both indoor under-sink units and outdoor ground-level tanks.

    The frequency of grease trap cleaning depends heavily on usage. Commercial kitchens often require cleaning every 1 to 3 months, while residential units might only need it once a year or less. Local Myrtle Beach health codes for restaurants may mandate specific intervals.

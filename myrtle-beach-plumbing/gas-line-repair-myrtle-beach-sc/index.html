    Natural Gas Leak Detection and Repair in Myrtle Beach

    Finding a gas leak starts at the meter. With every appliance off, a moving test dial on the meter tells us gas is escaping somewhere, and then we isolate branches to narrow it down. A combustible gas detector narrows it further. A spray of soap solution on the fitting shows bubbles at the leak, and we fix that joint or section and retest the whole system.

    Cost of Gas Line Repair in Myrtle Beach, SC

    Gas Leak Repair at Fittings and Valves in Myrtle Beach

    Most residential gas leaks are at a threaded joint, a valve stem or a flexible connector, not in the middle of a pipe. That keeps repairs small. We back out the leaking joint, clean the threads, apply gas-rated thread sealant and reassemble it, or replace the fitting or valve if the threads are damaged. Every repair gets a soap test and a system pressure test before we leave.

    Permits and Code Compliance for Gas Work in Myrtle Beach, SC

    Most gas piping work beyond a simple connector swap needs a permit in Myrtle Beach, SC. Inspection covers sizing, approved materials, pipe supports, shutoffs, drip legs and a witnessed or documented pressure test. The permit is on us. Doing it this way keeps your insurance clean and gives you a record of the repair if you sell the house later.

    Corroded Gas Pipe Replacement in Myrtle Beach

    Buried steel gas line corrodes once its coating cracks, and moisture, roots and ground movement speed it up. Red clay dominates much of the upstate, while the coastal plain of South Carolina has sandy soil and a shallow water table that affects drainage and digging. We replace corroded sections with polyethylene pipe laid with tracer wire, using an approved transition riser where the line comes up out of the ground at the house. Above-ground runs stay in steel. We test before backfilling so any problem joint is fixed while the trench is still open.

    Repairs that change piping, and all new gas lines, usually need a permit and inspection from Myrtle Beach building officials. After it passes, the gas company can restore service, and we can return to relight the pilots.

    Sump Pump Types We Replace in Mount Pleasant, SC

    Wayne Sump Pump Replacement in Mount Pleasant

    Wayne makes everything from a thermoplastic quarter-horse pump at the hardware store to cast iron models with vertical switches, and the cheap ones are what we replace most. Most dead Wayne pumps we see have a cooked motor or a float tangled in the power cord. You get a matching Wayne or a sturdier cast iron model, your call. Takes about two hours.

    Submersible Sump Pump Replacement in Mount Pleasant

    A submersible pump sits at the bottom of the pit, sealed in an oil-filled housing, and it runs quieter than anything else you can put down there. Out comes the old pump, out comes the muck at the bottom of the basin, and the new one goes in on a brick to stay clear of silt. Then a new check valve goes on. Skip that part and the pump short cycles.

    High-Capacity Sump Pump Upgrades in Mount Pleasant

    If your pump runs every 30 seconds during a storm and still loses ground, it is undersized for the water coming in. We time the cycles and measure how fast the pit refills. From there it is math. A half-horse or three-quarter-horse pump with a higher gallons-per-minute rating at your lift height may be the answer, sometimes paired with a 2-inch discharge.

    Sump Basin and Pit Replacement in Mount Pleasant, SC

    Old clay tile or 18-inch plastic basins crack and fill with silt, and a pump in a basin that small cycles every few minutes during a heavy rain. We break out the concrete, set a larger poly basin with a sealed lid, and pour the floor back. A bigger basin means longer pump cycles. Fewer starts, longer motor life.

    Pedestal Pump Service in Mount Pleasant, SC

    Louder, yes, but a pedestal motor sits high and dry, so in a narrow pit it often outlasts a submersible by several years. Narrow pits favor pedestals. We replace worn units and set the float rod stops for a proper cycle. If your pit is wide enough, a submersible may serve you better.

    Light Commercial Sump Pump Replacement in Mount Pleasant, SC

    Light commercial work, think retail basements and small office buildings, usually means a 1/2 HP or larger cast iron pump with a 2-inch discharge and a panel alarm. We replace like for like or upsize. Grinder and ejector pumps are a separate job.

    Pedestal Pumps for Shallow Pits in Mount Pleasant, SC

    Older basements sometimes have a narrow, shallow pit poured right into the floor, and a pedestal pump is often the only thing that fits without breaking concrete. We swap the pump, replace the float rod and stops, and run new 1.5-inch PVC to the check valve. Cheaper than a submersible, too.

    Battery Backup Sump Pump Installation in Mount Pleasant

    Coastal areas deal with storm surge and a high water table, while inland areas see sudden flooding from slow-moving summer thunderstorms. A standard pump just sits there once the power drops. The fix is a second, 12-volt pump on its own float, set a little higher than the main pump and wired to a charger and deep-cycle battery. It also covers a stuck primary switch. The alarm will tell you which one is running.

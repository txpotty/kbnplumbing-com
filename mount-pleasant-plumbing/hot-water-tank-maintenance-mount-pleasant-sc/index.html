    Sediment Flush and Descaling in Mount Pleasant

    Hard water leaves calcium carbonate behind every time the burner heats the tank. Over a year that layer can harden into chunks that insulate the water from the flame and cause noisy popping. We connect a hose to the drain valve, flush with full line pressure and agitate the bottom until the water runs clear. When scale is severe we use a descaling solution approved for potable systems. A clean tank heats faster and puts less stress on the bottom seam.

    A standard flush and inspection usually runs $125 to $250 depending on tank size and access. Anode replacement usually adds $75 to $200 in parts and labor, and powered anodes cost more.

    Private wells are common outside town limits, and some South Carolina well water carries iron, low pH or sediment that can stain fixtures and wear down water heaters.     Neglecting maintenance can lead to inefficient operation, higher energy bills, premature tank failure from corrosion or sediment buildup, and potential safety hazards from a malfunctioning pressure relief valve. Regular service heads off these costly and dangerous issues.

    That sound is water trapped under a layer of sediment boiling as the burner fires. A flush usually quiets it, though very old hardened scale may not come out completely.

    The anode rod, also known as a sacrificial rod, is a metal rod inside your hot water tank designed to corrode before the tank itself. It protects the tank lining from rust and corrosion, which counts most when water carries a lot of minerals, and it lengthens the lifespan of your unit.

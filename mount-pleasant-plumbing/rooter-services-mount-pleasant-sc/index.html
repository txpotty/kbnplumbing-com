We provide upfront pricing after a thorough diagnosis, so you know the cost before any work begins.

    No permit is required for routine rooting. Installing a new cleanout or repairing pipe may require one. We handle permits for any repair work.

    Homes with tree roots or older clay pipe often need rooting every 12 to 24 months. Homes with newer PVC lines may go many years without it. A camera inspection can help set the right schedule.

    Snaking uses an auger to break through and pull out blockages, while hydro-jetting uses high-pressure water to scour the entire inside of the pipe, removing buildup like grease and mineral deposits. Hydro-jetting provides a far more thorough cleaning, often recommended for recurring clogs or main sewer lines.

    Rooting uses a spinning cable with cutting blades to break up clogs and roots. Hydro jetting uses high-pressure water to scrub the pipe walls. Many tough lines do best with both.

    Drain snaking, also known as augering, uses a long, flexible cable with a cutting head to physically break through or retrieve blockages like hair or tree roots. Hydro-jetting uses high-pressure water to clean the entire interior of the pipe, removing grease, mineral buildup, and sludge, leaving pipes much cleaner than snaking alone. Hydro-jetting is generally more effective for severe or recurring clogs.

    Rooting cuts roots back but does not seal the pipe joints, so roots will eventually regrow. Root treatments slow regrowth, and pipe lining or replacement provides a lasting fix.

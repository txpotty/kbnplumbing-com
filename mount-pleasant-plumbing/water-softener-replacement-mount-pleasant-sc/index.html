    Salt-Based Water Softener Replacement in Mount Pleasant, SC

    Dual Tank Softener Systems in Mount Pleasant

    Large households and homes with very hard water can benefit from a dual tank system that provides soft water all day and night. While one tank regenerates, the other stays in service. Kinetico and similar non-electric twin tank systems are popular for this reason. We size dual tank systems to your peak demand. They cost more upfront but avoid hard water bleed-through during regeneration.

    Brine Tank Replacement in Mount Pleasant

    Cracked brine tanks, salt bridges and failing float assemblies cause softeners to stop regenerating properly. We replace brine tanks and safety floats to restore consistent brine draw. We also clean out mushy salt buildup that clogs the tank. A new brine tank is often part of a full system replacement. We recommend the right salt type for your system.

    Resin Tank and Media Replacement in Mount Pleasant, SC

    Softener resin can break down after 10 to 15 years, especially with chlorinated city water. When resin fails, you will see hard water symptoms even though the system is regenerating. Sometimes replacing resin and the tank is more economical than a whole new system. We inspect the valve and brine tank to decide. If the valve is old, a full replacement often makes better sense.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Water Softener Replacement in Mount Pleasant

    The cost of water softener replacement in Mount Pleasant, SC can vary widely, typically ranging from $1,500 to $4,000. This depends on the type and size of the new system, labor costs and any needed plumbing changes. We provide transparent, upfront pricing after assessing your needs.

    The right size depends on your household's daily water usage and the hardness of your water in grains per gallon. Our technicians will perform a water test and calculate the appropriate softening capacity in grains per day for your Mount Pleasant home, so the system keeps up.

    Spots on dishes, scale on faucets, dry skin and increased soap use can point to a failing softener. A softener that no longer uses salt or keeps flooding the brine tank may also need replacement.

    Most softener replacements take two to four hours. New loops or drain lines can take a bit longer.

    Common signs include a return of hard water symptoms like scale buildup on fixtures, dry skin and hair, dingy laundry or heavy soap use. Other signs are a clear drop in water pressure, leaks from the unit, or a softener over 10 to 15 years old that needs frequent repairs.

    Salt-based softeners remove hardness minerals, calcium and magnesium, through ion exchange, truly softening the water. Salt-free conditioners do not remove minerals but alter their crystal structure so they do not stick to surfaces. That prevents scale buildup without actually softening the water.

    Most standard residential water softener replacements can be completed within 3 to 5 hours. This includes disconnecting the old unit, installing the new one, making plumbing connections and testing the system. More complex installations may take a bit longer.

    In many areas, a plumbing permit is generally not required for a direct like-for-like water softener replacement if no significant changes to the existing plumbing are made. However, if new water lines or drain lines are being installed or relocated, a permit may be required. We will advise you on any permitting requirements.

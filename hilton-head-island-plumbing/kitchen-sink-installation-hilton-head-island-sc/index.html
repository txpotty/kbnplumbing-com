    New Construction Sink Rough-In in Hilton Head Island, SC

    Rough-in sets the drain height and the stops long before the cabinets arrive.     Undermount Kitchen Sink Installation in Hilton Head Island

    Undermount sinks rely on clips and adhesive to stay attached to granite or quartz. The bowl is mounted, the rim sealed with silicone and the sink braced while the adhesive sets, normally overnight before it can take a full bowl of water. Strainers and traps go in after.

    Apron-Front Sinks in Hilton Head Island, SC

    Apron sinks need a cabinet cut to fit the front panel, and the bowl must be set before the counter gets measured. We set the sink and frame early, so the fabricator measures from the actual bowl instead of a spec sheet. That avoids an uneven reveal. Cast iron versions can top 150 pounds, so we bring two people.

    Disposal and Dishwasher Hookup with Sink Installation in Hilton Head Island, SC

    Most new kitchen sinks get paired with a disposal and a dishwasher. The disposal gets mounted, the dishwasher drain goes through an air gap or high loop, and the dishwasher gets its own hot line and shutoff. One visit costs less than three. We test it all the way you will use it.

    Workstation Sink Installation in Hilton Head Island, SC

    Workstation sinks have a ledge for cutting boards, colanders and drying racks, and most are single deep bowls with an offset drain. The trap layout follows the offset drain, which lets the disposal fit and leaves storage room on one side. Check the faucet reach as well.

    Undermount Sinks on Stone in Hilton Head Island, SC

    Every fabricator handles undermount clips a bit differently. Some epoxy threaded studs into the stone, others use a rail system, and we work with whichever one your countertop shop prefers. Either way, the bowl gets braced. The trap does not get connected until the adhesive cures.

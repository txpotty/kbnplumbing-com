    What Goes Into a Sewer Replacement in Aiken, SC

    Trenchless Pipe Bursting in Aiken

    Brittle pipe bursts cleanly. A hydraulic rig at the receiving pit pulls the bursting head through the old line, splitting it into the soil, while a continuous length of fused HDPE follows behind which leaves a single joint-free pipe in the ground. Roots cannot get into a joint that does not exist.

    Traditional Sewer Line Replacement in Aiken

    Digging is still the right method for a lot of lines. A collapsed pipe cannot guide a bursting head. Sharp bends, a belly that needs regrading, or a route that should change all point toward an open trench, and we leave the yard as close to how we found it as dirt allows. Grass grows back.

    Clay and Orangeburg Pipe Replacement in Aiken, SC

    Clay pipe came in short sections with a joint every two or three feet. Each of those joints is an opening for roots. Orangeburg is worse, since the tar-paper wall softens and deforms under the weight of the soil, and both are good candidates for bursting because they break apart easily. Fast pulls.

    Residential Sewer Replacement in Aiken, SC

    Most house laterals run 30 to 100 feet. Some are two feet down, some are eight. We measure both before quoting, check whether the run passes under a driveway, porch or tree, and plan the method around what sits on top of the pipe, so the estimate you sign is the price you pay.

    Foundation Protection during Sewer Replacement in Aiken, SC

    Red clay dominates much of the upstate, while the coastal plain of South Carolina has sandy soil and a shallow water table that affects drainage and digging. A sewer leaking under or beside the house keeps the soil wet in one spot, and that uneven moisture can push or settle part of a slab. When we replace a line near the foundation we dig carefully, avoid undermining footings, and compact the backfill in lifts. Slow work near the house.

    Code-Compliant Sewer Replacement in Aiken

    Code sets the minimum slope, the cleanout spacing and the pipe type. Inspectors check all three in an open trench. We set fall at a quarter inch per foot unless the run requires otherwise, place cleanouts at the house and at major direction changes, and only bury pipe rated for it. Paperwork stays on file.

    Sewer Pipe Material Upgrades in Aiken, SC

    Plenty of older houses still drain through their first sewer pipe. Clay joints open up and let roots in, cast iron scales and cracks from the inside, and Orangeburg, a tar-paper pipe from the mid 1900s, squashes into an oval over time. Replacement means PVC or HDPE.

    Open-Trench Sewer Replacement in Aiken, SC

    Trenching lets us fix grade. Correcting slope is where it shines. When an old line has a sag that holds water and solids, bursting would just follow the same sag, so we dig and reset the pipe on a proper bed at a proper fall. Bellies disappear.

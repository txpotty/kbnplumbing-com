    Propane Gas Leak Repair in Summerville, SC

    Propane behaves differently from natural gas because it is heavier than air, so a leak settles into basements and crawlspaces instead of rising. We check both regulators, the one at the tank and the one at the house. Low spots get sniffed first. Your propane supplier owns the tank in most cases, so we coordinate with them for anything on the tank side of the first regulator. Rural properties with buried tanks get a line test too.

    Residential Gas Leak Detection in Summerville

    A gas leak in a house rarely sits where the smell is strongest, because gas drifts along joists and collects in closets and basements. The first step is a pressure gauge on the piping, which tells us whether the system is actually losing gas. Then the sniffer comes out. A combustible gas detector follows the reading from fitting to fitting until it spikes. Bubble solution on the suspect joint gives the final answer.

    Commercial Gas Leak Repair in Summerville

    Commercial gas piping often runs at 2 psi or higher with regulators at each appliance bank, so a leak test there is not the same as a house test. We check regulator lockup and vent lines along with the joints. Rooftop units leak too. Where downtime costs you money, we isolate only the affected branch and keep the rest of the building running. Staff get a short walkthrough on the shutoff locations.

    Gas Leak Detection in Summerville

    Some leaks announce themselves with a hiss you can hear across the room, and others lose a few cubic feet a day and only show up on the bill. The slow ones are where the gauge earns its keep. We isolate, then sniff. We run the test twice, with appliance valves closed and then open, because some leaks only show downstream of a valve. Finding the exact joint keeps the repair small.

    Emergency Gas Leak Repair in Summerville, SC

    Hissing at the meter, a sulfur smell in the kitchen, a dead patch of lawn or a carbon monoxide alarm all mean the same thing: get everyone outside before you touch anything. Call the utility from outside, not from inside the house. KBN Plumbing runs emergency gas calls in Summerville, SC around the clock.     Permitting and Code Compliance in Summerville, SC

    Most jurisdictions want a permit for new gas pipe or a moved line, though a straight connector swap often does not need one. We tell you up front which way your job falls. The fee goes on the estimate. The work follows South Carolina adopted fuel gas code and any local amendments. When the inspector signs off, you get the paperwork, which matters later when the house is sold and a buyer's inspector asks about the gas work.

    Appliance Connector Replacement in Summerville

    Old uncoated brass connectors, the kind made before the mid-1970s, are the subject of consumer safety warnings because the flare nut can separate from the tube. If your range or water heater still has one, it should come out. We see them weekly. In its place goes a coated stainless connector and a new shutoff valve, plus a drip leg if the code calls for it. Bubble testing follows before the pilot is lit.

    Corrugated Stainless Steel Tubing Repair in Summerville, SC

    CSST is the yellow-jacketed flexible tubing used in many newer homes, and it can be damaged by a nearby lightning strike if it was never bonded. The bond is a heavy copper wire tying the gas pipe to the house ground. Plenty of older installs lack it. Repairs use the same brand's fittings as the existing tubing, since manufacturers do not allow mixing parts. A splice that skips that rule fails inspection.

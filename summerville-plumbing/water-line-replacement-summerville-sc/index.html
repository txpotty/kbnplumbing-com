    Main Water Line Replacement in Summerville

    Your service line runs from the meter or curb stop to the main shutoff inside the house, usually as 3/4-inch or 1-inch pipe. Once it has leaked in two different places, patching stops making sense. We dig at both ends, pull or cut out the old pipe, and run new line at the depth local code calls for, then tie into the meter and the house valve. Most homes are done in a day.

    Trenchless Bores Under Driveways in Summerville

    Directional boring drills a path under the obstacle and pulls the pipe back through it. It does not need the old pipe as a guide. We use it when the new line has to take a different route, say around a new addition or under a driveway poured after the house was built, leaving just two small pits in the yard. Heavy rock is the exception.

    Galvanized Main Line Replacement in Summerville

    Galvanized steel was common in homes built before the 1960s. Inside, the pipe rusts once the zinc is gone. After a few decades the opening can be half its original size, and that shows up as weak pressure and orange water, and no amount of cleaning brings it back. It has to come out.

    Traditional Water Line Excavation in Summerville

    Sometimes the open trench wins. If the old pipe has collapsed or turns hard corners, we dig. We cut the sod and set it aside, dig to depth with a mini excavator or by hand near other utilities, lay the new pipe on a sand bed, and backfill in layers with compaction so the trench does not sink after the first good rain. Sod goes back on top.

    Commercial Water Line Services in Summerville, SC

    Commercial services run bigger, often 1-1/2 to 2 inches, and almost always have a backflow assembly that needs testing after any work. Shutdowns get scheduled for when you are closed. For a restaurant or an office building that might mean an early morning start, temporary water to the restrooms, and coordination with the water utility on the meter side. Tenants get notice.

    Water Line Materials We Install in Summerville, SC

    Our trucks carry Type K copper, PEX-a and HDPE. Each one fits a different job. Copper suits short runs and high pressure, PEX handles long runs with bends, and HDPE is the usual pick for trenchless pulls and bores, where we need a tough, fused pipe that can be dragged a long way. All of it is listed for drinking water, carrying the NSF 61 listing.

    Damage Assessment Before Replacement in Summerville

    We start at the meter. With every tap closed, the leak indicator should sit still. From there we listen along the line with an acoustic ground mic, rule out a running toilet or water softener, and sometimes push a camera into the line when we suspect root damage or a crushed section, so the hole goes in the right spot.

    PEX Water Line Installation in Summerville, SC

    PEX bends around corners, so a typical service run needs no fittings between the meter and the house. Hard freezes are infrequent in South Carolina, but a few nights each winter can drop well below freezing, especially in the upstate. A frozen PEX line often survives where copper would crack. We still bury it below the frost depth the local code sets, and keep it covered from sunlight at the meter box, since UV light breaks it down. Short exposure only.

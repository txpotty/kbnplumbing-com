    Recirculation and Combi Unit Service in Greenville, SC

    Annual Tankless Checkup in Greenville, SC

    The annual checkup is a top-to-bottom pass on the heater. Gas pressure at the inlet gets read with a manometer at full fire and checked against the rating plate. We check the flue for blockages. You get a written report with the readings, so next year's visit has something to compare against.

    Inlet Screen and Air Filter Cleaning in Greenville

    Grit gets in. The small screen on the cold inlet catches sand and loose scale after a water main repair. Units with an air intake filter collect dust and lint, which can push the burner into a combustion code. We clean both on every visit.

    Rinnai Air Filter and Flame Rod Care in Greenville

    Rinnai cabinets are tight. We wash the air intake filter and check the flame rod for carbon. On recirculating models we also check the pump schedule, because a wrong setting burns gas for nothing.

    Heat Exchanger Descaling in Greenville

    Picture a bundle of thin tubes with a gas flame under them and cold water racing through at three or four gallons a minute. That is your heat exchanger. Calcium and magnesium drop out on the hottest tube walls first and narrow the passages. We dissolve it with 45 to 60 minutes of pump-driven descaling solution.

    Step-by-Step Tankless Flushing in Greenville

    First we shut off the gas and power, then close both isolation valves so the heater is cut off from the house plumbing. Solution goes from a bucket into the cold port, out the hot port and back to the bucket. After the cycle we rinse with clean water and pull the inlet screen. Then we check for leaks.

    Tankless Error Code Diagnosis in Greenville, SC

    An error code is the heater telling you what it saw. Rinnai LC codes point to scale, Navien E003 is a failed ignition and Noritz 90 points at combustion air or exhaust trouble. We pull the code history before touching anything. If a part has failed, you hear the repair and the price before we start.

    Home Tankless Flushing in Greenville

    Private wells are common outside town limits, and some South Carolina well water carries iron, low pH or sediment that can stain fixtures and wear down water heaters. Inside a tankless heater, that water runs through a small copper or stainless coil while the burner fires, and every minute of hot water leaves a little calcium behind. A pump on the service valves pushes descaling solution through the coil for roughly an hour. Flow comes back. Temperatures stop swinging, and the unit quits shutting down on high-limit.

    Accessible and ADA Shower Installation in Greenville, SC

    Accessible showers start with blocking in the wall. Blocking goes in for grab bars, the valve goes where a seated person can reach it and a handheld goes on a slide bar. Thresholds stay low or flat. ADA layouts are available on request.

    Tiled Walk-In Shower Installation in Greenville

    Frameless glass walk-ins are popular, and the plumbing behind them has to be dead on. Valve, head, handheld and drain go in first, then the pan is sloped a quarter inch per foot to the drain. Testing follows. The valve gets a pressure test and the pan a 24-hour flood test before tile.

    Steam Shower Plumbing in Greenville

    Steam showers need four things: a generator, a water supply, a drain line and an electrical circuit. The generator goes within the maker's distance limit, and we plumb its supply, drain and steam line to the head. The circuit is an electrician's job. A sealed enclosure with a sloped ceiling keeps drips off you.

    Shower Pan Installation in Greenville

    Shower pans fail quietly. Pinholes and loose drain clamps let water into the subfloor slowly, which is why tiled pans sit flooded for a full day before tile. Prefab pans get a level check. Both kinds get slope toward the drain.

    Gym and Locker Room Showers in Greenville

    Gym showers run back to back from open to close. Commercial valves with metered or push-button controls keep run times in check, and the drains are sized for heavy use. ADA stalls get handheld units. Work is phased so part of the room stays open.

    Shower Stall Installation in Greenville

    A one-piece stall needs a level, fully supported base. We set fiberglass and acrylic bases in a bed of mortar or setting compound so the floor of the unit does not flex underfoot, since flex is what cracks a base within a couple of years. Then the drain goes in. Flow and temperature get tested after trim.

    Tub-to-Shower Conversion in Greenville, SC

    Tub-to-shower conversions are one of our most common jobs. We pull the tub, move the drain from the end of the tub to where the shower pan needs it and raise the valve to shower height, usually around 48 inches off the floor. On a slab, we cut concrete. The new shower is safer to step into.

    Digital Shower Systems in Greenville, SC

    Digital shower systems like U by Moen and Kohler DTV need a little more planning. These valves usually sit in a nearby closet or vanity and need low-voltage wiring to the controller and an outlet close by. An electrician handles the outlet. Our part is the plumbing.

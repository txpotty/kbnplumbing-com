    Sump Pump Pit Installation in Greenville, SC

    A properly built sump pit is fundamental to an effective sump pump system. If your basement lacks a suitable pit or needs an upgrade, KBN Plumbing provides professional sump pit installation. We carefully excavate and prepare the pit to the correct dimensions and depth for proper drainage and pump function. This first step is critical for any new sump pump installation and prevents the sediment buildup that can damage the pump.

    Sump Pump Basin and Discharge Line Installation in Greenville

    A complete sump pump system requires a properly installed basin and discharge line. We handle the excavation and placement of the sump basin, setting it at the lowest point of your basement floor so it collects water effectively. We also install the discharge line, making sure it is correctly sloped, sealed and routed away from your foundation to a suitable drainage area, so water does not find its way back into your Greenville property.

    Pedestal Sump Pumps in Greenville

    Pedestal pumps keep the motor above the pit, which makes them easier to service and a solid fit for narrow pits in older homes. They typically last a long time because the motor never sits in water. The tradeoff is more noise and a less finished look, so we generally recommend them for utility rooms rather than living spaces. We install them with a rigid discharge, a quality check valve and a float rod set to your pit depth.

    Wayne Sump Pump Installation in Greenville, SC

    Wayne sump pumps offer a range of options, from basic utility pumps to advanced systems with built-in alarms and smart features. Our technicians are familiar with installing Wayne pumps, providing efficient water removal for your Greenville, SC basement.     Battery Backup Sump Pump Systems in Greenville

    Power outages during heavy storms can render a primary sump pump useless, leading to basement flooding. A battery backup sump pump provides crucial protection when the power goes out. We install dependable backup systems that activate automatically, keeping water moving even without electricity. This is an essential safeguard for homes in Greenville that lose power during storms, offering peace of mind in severe weather. We tie these systems cleanly with your existing plumbing.

    New Sump Pit Excavation in Greenville

    If your basement has never had a pump, we start by locating the low point where water collects. Gravel goes around the liner to help water flow in while keeping fine soil out. We then patch the slab flush with new concrete so the floor stays clean and level.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Basement Sump Pump Installation in Greenville

    Replacing a pump in an existing pit typically runs $500 to $1,200 including the pump and check valve. Installing a new pit through the slab generally falls between $1,500 and $3,500, and adding a battery backup adds about $600 to $1,500.

    A primary sump pump is your main defense against basement flooding, built to handle everyday water removal. A secondary sump pump, often a battery-powered backup, serves as an emergency system that activates if the primary pump fails, loses power or is overwhelmed by excess water. Both work together for maximum protection.

    While it might seem like a DIY project, basement sump pump installation involves real plumbing, electrical work and proper discharge routing. Incorrect installation can lead to pump failure, electrical hazards or poor water removal, potentially causing serious flood damage. It is always best to hire a licensed plumber for a proper, code-compliant installation in Greenville.

    Common signs you might need a new sump pump include unusual noises during operation, visible rust or corrosion on the unit, frequent cycling on and off, or a pump that fails to turn on during heavy rain. If your pump is more than 7 to 10 years old, it is also smart to consider a replacement before it fails completely.

    Most residential basements need a 1/3 or 1/2 horsepower pump, but the right answer depends on how fast water enters the pit and how high it has to be lifted. We measure both before recommending a model.

    Swapping a pump in an existing pit usually takes two to three hours. A new pit with slab cutting and concrete patching usually takes most of a day.

    We recommend having your sump pump inspected and maintained annually, ideally before the rainy season. Regular maintenance includes checking the float switch, cleaning debris out of the pit, testing the check valve and confirming the discharge line is clear. This helps prevent unexpected failures and extends the life of your pump.

    Usually, standard home insurance policies do not cover a new sump pump installation or repairs unless the damage was caused by a sudden, accidental event like a burst pipe, rather than gradual wear and tear or groundwater seepage. Some policies offer separate water backup or sump pump failure endorsements for an additional premium. It is best to check with your insurance provider.

    Foundation Protection during Sewer Replacement in Florence, SC

    Clay that swells when wet and shrinks in dry spells can stress buried sewer lines over time, leading to sags, separated joints and root intrusion. A sewer leaking under or beside the house keeps the soil wet in one spot, which can lead to uneven settling of the slab. When we replace a line near the foundation we dig carefully, avoid undermining footings, and tamp each layer of fill. Slow work near the house.

    Residential Sewer Replacement in Florence, SC

    Most house laterals run 30 to 100 feet. Depth varies a lot. We measure both before quoting, check whether the run passes under a driveway, porch or tree, then pick trench or trenchless based on what is in the way, so the estimate you sign is the price you pay.

    Traditional Sewer Line Replacement in Florence

    Digging is still the right method for a lot of lines. If the old pipe has caved in, trenchless has nothing to follow. Sharp bends, a belly that needs regrading, or a route that should change all point toward an open trench, and we restore the sod or gravel afterward. Grass grows back.

    Traditional Trench Sewer Replacement in Florence

    An open trench gives full access to the pipe from the house to the main. We dig with a mini excavator and hand dig near utility marks. The old pipe comes out, the trench bottom gets a bed of gravel or sand, and new 4-inch PVC goes in at a steady quarter inch of fall per foot, checked with a level before backfill. Then the camera.

    Addressing Tree Root Intrusions in Florence, SC

    Roots find sewer lines by following moisture. They need only a tiny gap. Once inside, they grow into a mat that catches paper and grease, and a root cutter clears them for a season at most, because the opening is still there. New PVC with glued joints gives them no way in.

    Clay and Orangeburg Pipe Replacement in Florence, SC

    Clay pipe came in short sections with a joint every two or three feet. Each of those joints is an opening for roots. Orangeburg is worse, since the tar-paper wall softens and deforms under the weight of the soil, and both are good candidates for bursting because they break apart easily. Fast pulls.

    Trenchless Pipe Bursting in Florence

    Brittle pipe bursts cleanly. A hydraulic rig at the receiving pit pulls the bursting head through the old line, splitting it into the soil, while a continuous length of fused HDPE follows behind which leaves a single joint-free pipe in the ground. Roots cannot get into a joint that does not exist.

    Sewer Line Repair vs. Full Replacement in Florence

    We look at the whole line before recommending either. A single bad joint can usually be dug and fixed. Breaks every few feet, roots at every joint, or a belly that holds water for twenty feet mean the rest of the pipe is close behind, and in that case a full replacement saves you from paying to dig the same yard twice.

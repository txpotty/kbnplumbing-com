    Frequently Asked Questions About Grease Trap Cleaning in Florence

    Small under-sink traps often start around $200 a visit. Large outdoor interceptors can pass $1,000 when they are full and the cap has hardened. The biggest variable is the gallons hauled, because disposal facilities charge by volume and a 1,500-gallon tank is a full truckload.

    Everything in the trap comes out, from the floating grease cap down to the sludge of food solids on the floor. The industry calls it brown grease. Yellow grease, the used fryer oil in your collection bin, is a separate product that gets recycled and should never go into the trap.

    FOG stands for fats, oils and grease. It pours as a liquid and sets up hard once it cools. Inside a sewer pipe it builds up in layers on the walls, traps food and wipes, and can shrink the opening to the size of a pencil.

    Staff can clean a small under-sink trap between pump-outs. Disposal still has to be legal. A common setup is daily skimming by staff and a full pump and scrape from us every few weeks, so the records stay complete.

    The terms get mixed up constantly. Generally a trap is the small unit indoors, often under 100 gallons. An interceptor is the big in-ground tank outside, often 500 gallons or more, and plumbing codes treat them as separate categories with different sizing rules.

    It goes to a permitted receiving facility. Some facilities process it for biofuel or digesters. What is left gets dewatered and disposed of under the facility's permit, and each load is logged from your trap to their gate.

    Short term, slow drains and smell. Longer term, a hardened grease cap that can take hours to break up. Hydrogen sulfide from the rotting grease also attacks concrete, so interceptors that go unpumped for years often need new baffles and risers well before their time.

    Rules come from your sewer district or city pretreatment program. Most follow some version of the 25 percent rule. Many also set a maximum interval, require records on site for a year or more, and ban enzyme additives as a substitute for pumping, so we check the program that covers your address before setting a schedule.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Drain Repairs in Charleston

    A simple clog clearing starts around $150. Bigger repairs climb fast. Complex main line repairs can run past $3,000, with the type of drain, how bad the damage is and how hard the pipe is to reach setting most of the price. You get a written number after diagnosis.

    Tightening a slip nut or replacing a plastic trap is fair game. Hand snakes are fine too. Anything inside a wall, under a floor or involving cast iron, or a repeat leak at the same joint, should go to a plumber. Chemical cleaners can make things worse.

    Clearing a simple clog takes an hour or two. Repairs run longer. Replacing a section of main line or opening a wall to swap a cast iron stack can take a full day or more, depending on access and whether a permit inspection is needed. The quote includes a time estimate.

    Fixing the cause instead of the clog. Slope matters most. After we correct a sag, replace a rough fitting or add a missing vent, the drain should run clean as long as grease and wipes stay out of it. Older lines may still need yearly cleaning.

    Most trap and fixture drain repairs take one to two hours. Wall work takes longer. Replacing drain pipe inside a wall or under a floor can take half a day to a full day, depending on how much finish material has to come out. The estimate includes the timeline.

    Snugging a slip nut or replacing a plastic trap is a fair DIY job. Do not crank it down. If the leak comes from a cracked tailpiece, a corroded metal drain or somewhere inside the wall, it needs a plumber who can fix the pipe and look for rot nearby. Water damage under sinks spreads quietly.

    We install PVC and ABS for most new drain work. Some jobs call for cast iron. Cast iron still makes sense in some multi-story walls where noise matters, and we tie new pipe into existing metal drains with listed transition fittings. All materials are code-listed.

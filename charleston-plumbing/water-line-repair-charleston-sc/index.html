    Main Water Line Repair in Charleston

    Your main line is the pipe from the meter to the house, usually 3/4 inch or 1 inch, buried anywhere from a foot to several feet down depending on frost depth. A leak here shows up as a soggy strip of lawn and a meter dial that never rests. We start by closing the house shutoff and watching the dial. A moving dial with the house valve closed means an underground leak. Then a ground microphone narrows the spot, and we dig one hole instead of a trench across your yard.

    PEX and Copper Water Line Repair in Charleston

    Copper and PEX fail in different places. Copper tends to pinhole where a rock rubs it or where the water chemistry eats the wall from inside. With PEX, the fitting is the weak point most of the time. We cut out the bad section of copper and sweat or press in a new length, while PEX gets a fresh crimp ring or expansion fitting with the right tool for the brand.

    Commercial Water Line Services in Charleston, SC

    Besides repairs, KBN Plumbing handles service line upgrades when a commercial tenant needs more flow, for example when a restaurant adds fixtures or a gym adds showers. Bigger meters need bigger pipe. We size the line from the fixture count. Then we coordinate with the water provider on the tap and meter change, set the backflow assembly and get the inspection signed off, so the space can open on schedule.

    Slab Leak Water Line Repair in Charleston

    A slab leak is a supply pipe leaking under the concrete floor of the house. Much of the housing in South Carolina was built after 1970, so copper, CPVC and PEX supply lines are far more common than old galvanized pipe. Signs include a warm spot on the floor, the sound of running water and a meter that moves with everything off. We pinpoint the leak with acoustic gear and thermal imaging. From there you have three choices: break the slab at the spot, reroute the line through the walls or attic, or abandon the old pipe under the floor and run new PEX above it. We price each one for you.

    Pressure Regulator and Shutoff Repair in Charleston

    High static pressure shortens the life of the service line and every fixture behind it. The usual code limit is 80 psi. We gauge the pressure at a hose bib, and if it reads high we replace the pressure reducing valve with a Watts or Zurn Wilkins unit. Old gate valves that will not close get replaced with full-port ball valves. That way you can actually shut the water off.

    Pipe Bursting and Pulling in Charleston

    Pipe bursting means splitting the old line in place while the new one follows behind it. It works best on straight runs. A cone-shaped head breaks the old pipe outward, and a length of HDPE trails behind it in the same path. You end up with a fresh line and two small pits, and the lawn, sidewalk and driveway in between stay where they are, which is the whole reason people pick this method over a trench.

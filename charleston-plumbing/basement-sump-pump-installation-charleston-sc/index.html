    Battery Backup Sump Pump Systems in Charleston

    Outages and heavy rain tend to show up together. A battery backup keeps a second pump running without house power, and a fully charged 75 amp-hour battery can run a typical backup pump for several hours of on-and-off cycling, longer on a light night. We load test the battery.

    Discharge Line and Check Valve Installation in Charleston

    Rigid PVC carries the water from the pump out through the rim joist and at least 10 feet from the house. We add a spring check valve so the water in the pipe cannot fall back, which cuts motor run time and the loud thunk you hear after every cycle. Hard freezes are infrequent in South Carolina, but a few nights each winter can drop well below freezing, especially in the upstate. We fit a freeze-resistant outlet.

    Battery Backup Sump Pump Installation in Charleston, SC

    Battery backups earn their keep during outages. A backwater valve, a working sump pump where one exists and clear yard drains go a long way toward safeguarding a home during storm season. A second pump goes into the same pit on its own float switch, wired to a sealed AGM battery on a smart charger that tests itself and beeps when the battery gets weak. The main pump keeps doing the daily work.

    Same-Day Sump Pump Installation in Charleston

    Same-day emergency installs cover pumps that quit in the middle of a storm. First we get the standing water out of the pit, then set a new pump on a new check valve, plug it into a tested GFCI outlet and watch it run through several cycles before leaving your Charleston basement. Then we talk about a backup.

    Zoeller Sump Pump Installation in Charleston

    Zoeller's M53 Mighty-Mate is the cast iron pump we install more than any other. It runs at 3/10 horsepower on a vertical float switch, and it moves about 2,580 gallons an hour at 5 feet of lift, enough for a lot of Charleston basements. Replacement switches are on every supply shelf.

    Sump Pit Installation in Charleston, SC

    Pit size controls how often the pump starts. Too small a pit means the pump short cycles in heavy flow, so we usually install an 18 by 22 inch or 18 by 30 inch basin with a solid bottom and perforated sides wrapped in filter fabric. Less cycling, longer life.

    Commercial and Multi-Unit Sump Installations in Charleston

    Duplex systems with two pumps and an alternating panel are standard for parking garages and multi-unit buildings with below-grade levels in Charleston. Each cycle, the panel switches which pump leads, so wear stays even. A high water alarm alerts building staff. We coordinate the wiring with your electrician.

    Quiet Submersible Pumps for Finished Basements in Charleston

    Finished basements call for a quiet pump and a sealed lid. A submersible with a gasketed cover keeps the noise down, and we route the discharge behind framing or through a closet so the pipe does not run across a wall you just painted in your Charleston home. We patch the slab flush.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Basement Sump Pump Installation in Charleston

    Replacing a pump in an existing pit usually runs $500 to $1,200 including the pump and check valve. A brand new pit through the slab falls between $1,500 and $3,500, and a battery backup adds roughly $600 to $1,500. Most people add the backup.

    Look for a pump that runs nonstop, short cycles, or does not start when the pit fills. Corrosion on the housing and a float that hangs up are warning signs, and if your basement flooded even though you have a pump, the pump or its switch was not up to the job. Over seven years old is suspect.

    Submersibles sit in the pit, sealed, and run quiet. Pedestal pumps keep the motor up on a column above the water, which makes them cheaper and easier to service but louder, so they work best in an unfinished utility room rather than a finished family room. Either type gets the job done.

    Zoeller, Wayne, Little Giant and Liberty Pumps cover most of our installs. Horsepower and lift decide which model goes in, then by features like a vertical float or a built-in alarm, so the pump fits your pit, your budget and the amount of water your Charleston basement actually takes on. Cast iron is our default.

    Grinding or rattling during a cycle is the first warning. Rust on the housing and constant on-off cycling are others, along with a pump that does not start during a heavy rain even though the pit is full to the float. Past ten years, replace it.

    Quality cast iron pumps usually carry three to five years of factory coverage. We back our installation labor separately, and we walk you through the exact terms for the model you pick before we install it.

    A straight pump replacement usually does not need a permit. New pits, new discharge routes and new circuits may, so we check the local rules and pull permits when they apply.

    Permit rules vary by town, including Charleston. Replacing a pump in the same pit rarely needs one, but a new pit, a new outlet or a new exit through the foundation wall often does, and we sort that out before any concrete gets cut. We handle the paperwork.

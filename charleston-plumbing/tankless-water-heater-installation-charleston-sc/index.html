    What Goes Into a Tankless Conversion in Charleston, SC

    Gas Tankless Water Heater Installation in Charleston

    Gas tankless units in Charleston are the common pick for whole-house hot water because a single 199,000 BTU burner can feed two showers and a sink at the same time without losing temperature. The catch is gas supply. Most homes need the line upsized from the meter, often from half-inch to three-quarter inch or larger. We size it and pressure test it before the unit is lit.

    Tankless Water Heater Relocation in Charleston

    Moving the heater is common on a tankless job since the tank's location may not suit the new unit. A garage or outside wall near the gas meter can shorten the gas run and simplify venting, but it may lengthen the hot water run to the bathrooms. We weigh both. Long hot runs may call for recirculation.

    Navien Tankless Water Heater Installation in Charleston

    Navien builds condensing tankless units with stainless heat exchangers, and many models include a built-in recirculation pump. The built-in pump can move hot water toward a distant bath on a timer, cutting the wait at the tap without a separate pump. Navien vents in PVC. There is a condensate line to route as well.

    Recirculation Systems for Tankless Heaters in Charleston

    Tankless heaters can take a few extra seconds to get hot water to a far bathroom, because the unit has to sense flow and fire first. A recirculation pump on a timer or a motion sensor keeps the hot line primed, so the wait at the farthest sink drops from a minute to a few seconds. It saves water too.

    Residential Tankless Water Heater Installation in Charleston

    Sizing a home unit comes down to two numbers: peak gallons per minute and temperature rise. A shower head flows about 2 gallons per minute and a kitchen faucet a bit less, so a house that runs two showers and a sink together needs roughly 5 to 6 gallons at the rise your inlet water calls for. It shortens the list quickly.

    Commercial Tankless Water Heater Installation in Charleston

    Commercial hot water loads in Charleston are heavier and more constant than residential. A restaurant can run a dish machine, hand sinks and a mop sink together, and the rack must hold temperature through the rush. We rack units with a common manifold. One can be serviced while the rest run.

    Rinnai, Navien and Noritz Installation in Charleston, SC

    We install Rinnai, Navien, Noritz and Rheem in Charleston, SC homes all year long. Rinnai has a wide model range and good parts support, Navien builds in recirculation on many models, Noritz makes compact condensing units that fit tight spaces, and Rheem pairs well with their tank lineup. Each warranty differs. We walk you through them.

    Propane Tankless Water Heater Installation in Charleston

    Propane homes in Charleston are good candidates for tankless, using a model built or converted for propane. Propane runs at a higher pressure than natural gas, so the regulator, gas line sizing and the unit's gas valve all have to match, and the propane supplier may need to change the regulator at the tank. A small tank can run short.

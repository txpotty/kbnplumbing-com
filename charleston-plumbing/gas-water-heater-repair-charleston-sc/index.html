    Pilot Light Repair in Charleston

    The thermocouple is the first thing we test when a pilot will not stay lit. A millivolt meter tells us in about a minute whether it is producing enough current. If it reads fine, the next suspects are a clogged pilot orifice, a cracked piezo wire or a thermal cutoff that tripped because the combustion chamber overheated from blocked air. Most of these jobs run about an hour.

    Tank Leaks and Anode Rods in Charleston

    Corrosion starts at the anode rod and works its way into the steel once the rod is spent. Even with softer supply water in Charleston, a yearly water heater flush still helps clear the sediment that settles in the bottom of the tank. We pull the anode, check how much magnesium or aluminum is left and replace it if the core wire is showing. A fresh anode, a drained tank and a relief valve that seats properly can add years to a heater in a Charleston home that has not started leaking from the shell. Water from the shell seam is another story. At that point the tank has to go.

    Power Vent Water Heater Repair in Charleston

    Power vent heaters push exhaust through PVC with a blower on top of the tank. When one quits, it is usually the blower motor, the pressure switch or the hot surface igniter. Condensate pooling in a vent with the wrong slope can also trip the pressure switch, so we check the pipe run all the way to the termination before we sign off on a part. We read the error code first. Then each part gets a meter test.

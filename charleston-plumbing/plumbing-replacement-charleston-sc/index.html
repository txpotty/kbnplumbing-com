    Water Heater Replacement in Charleston

    Water heaters in Charleston that leak from the tank, rumble on every heat cycle or run out of hot water halfway through a shower are usually cheaper to replace than to keep repairing. A leak from the tank body itself cannot be fixed. New units get matched for fuel and size, with venting updates plus an expansion tank or pan if code requires it. Most swaps take a few hours.

    Pipe Replacement & Whole-Home Repiping in Charleston, SC

    Homes in Charleston, SC with galvanized steel or polybutylene usually show it the same ways: low pressure, rusty water and leaks that keep coming back. Repiping swaps every supply line from the main to each fixture for PEX or copper, usually in two to four days with water restored most nights. We patch every opening.

    Tankless Water Heater Replacement in Charleston, SC

    Switching to tankless during a replacement is common in Charleston, SC, but it is a bigger job than a tank swap. The gas draw can be four times a 40-gallon tank, so the meter line usually gets upsized, and the venting moves from B-vent to stainless or PVC. We price both up front.

    Whole-Home Pipe Replacement in Charleston

    Several pinhole leaks, galvanized pipe or polybutylene in a Charleston home usually mean it is better off with a complete repipe. New PEX or copper often runs through the attic and down interior walls, letting us abandon leaking under-slab pipe instead of breaking concrete. The slab stays intact.

    Sump Pump and Well Pump Replacement in Charleston, SC

    A dead sump pump during a storm floods a basement fast, and a dead well pump means no water at all. In Charleston, SC, sump pumps get sized for inflow with a check valve and an optional battery backup, while well pumps get matched to depth, flow and switch settings. Both get tested before we go.

    Well Pump & Pressure Tank Replacement in Charleston

    Rural homes around Charleston that run on a well have no water at all when the pump quits. We pull the old submersible pump, check the wire and drop pipe for damage, set the new pump at the right depth and test flow and pressure, often replacing the pressure tank and switch at the same visit. Water is usually back that day.

    Repair vs Replacement Cost Comparison in Charleston, SC

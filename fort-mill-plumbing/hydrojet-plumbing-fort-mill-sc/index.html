    Frequently Asked Questions About Hydrojet Plumbing in Fort Mill

    The cost of hydrojet plumbing in Fort Mill, SC can range from $300 to $800 or more, depending on the severity and location of the clog, as well as the length and diameter of the pipe.     For most homes in Fort Mill, hydrojetting every 1 to 3 years can be helpful as preventive maintenance, especially if you have an older home, many mature trees, or frequently experience slow drains. Businesses with high-volume plumbing, like restaurants, may benefit from quarterly or semi-annual hydrojetting to prevent grease buildup.

    Repeat clogs, slow drains throughout the house, gurgling sounds and sewer odors all suggest buildup in the line. Kitchens that back up every few months are a classic sign of grease. A camera inspection confirms whether jetting is the right fix.

    Snaking involves using an auger to punch a hole through a clog, usually leaving behind some debris. Hydrojetting, on the other hand, uses high-pressure water to thoroughly scour the entire inner surface of the pipe, removing buildup and leaving it clean. Hydrojetting provides a much more comprehensive and longer-lasting solution.

    Most homes only need jetting every few years or when clogs recur. Restaurants and busy commercial kitchens often benefit from jetting every three to six months. We recommend a schedule based on what the camera reveals.

    Hydrojetting is generally safe for most modern plumbing pipes, including PVC, ABS, and cast iron, when performed by a skilled professional. However, it may not be suitable for severely corroded, cracked, or improperly installed pipes. We always conduct a pre-inspection to confirm pipe integrity.

    The duration of hydrojet plumbing depends on the complexity of the clog and the length of the pipe being cleaned. Most residential hydrojetting jobs in Fort Mill can be completed within 1 to 3 hours, but more severe or extensive blockages, especially those involving main sewer lines or heavy root intrusion, may take longer.

    It is safe for most cast iron, clay, ABS and PVC lines in decent condition. We always inspect with a camera first and lower the pressure or recommend another method if the pipe is cracked or badly corroded. That step protects weak lines.

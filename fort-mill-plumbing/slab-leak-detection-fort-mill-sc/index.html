    How We Find Slab Leaks in Fort Mill, SC

    Pressure Testing and Isolation in Fort Mill

    We always start with isolation. Our tech closes the main, caps or valves off the hot and cold sides, and puts a gauge on each, then watches which one drops over fifteen to thirty minutes. That tells us where to look. It also rules out a toilet or faucet leak masquerading as a slab leak in your Fort Mill, SC home.

    Supply and Drain Leaks Under the Slab in Fort Mill, SC

    A supply leak and a drain leak show different signs. Supply leaks are pressurized, so they move the meter, warm the floor on the hot side and run whether or not anyone uses water. Drain leaks only leak when water goes down, and they show up as odors, slow drains or damp floors near bathrooms. Each needs its own test in your Fort Mill, SC home.

    Listening Through Concrete in Fort Mill

    Ground microphones are sensitive. That means appliances, HVAC fans and traffic all show up in the headphones, so our techs shut off what they can and learn to tune out the rest, which is a skill that takes years to get good at. Pressurized lines are the easiest to hear in your Fort Mill home.

    Cold Water Slab Leak Detection in Fort Mill, SC

    Cold-side leaks leave no warm spot. They are harder to see. They usually show up as a running meter, damp carpet, a musty smell or water seeping at baseboards, which is why we isolate the cold line, trace its route and listen along it with acoustic gear.

    Repiping After Repeat Slab Leaks in Fort Mill, SC

    A second slab leak is often a sign of more. The rest of the pipe has likely aged the same way, so repiping replaces every supply line with new PEX or copper through the walls and attic, which ends the pattern of new leaks and new floor openings. A full repipe takes about two to four days.

    Hot Water Slab Leak Detection in Fort Mill

    The hot side fails more than the cold. Heat speeds up corrosion, and the pipe expands and contracts every time someone showers, wearing against the gravel and concrete until a pinhole opens. Warm floors and a water heater that runs nonstop are the signs. Energy bills often rise.

    Thermal Imaging for Slab Leaks in Fort Mill, SC

    A leaking hot line heats the concrete over it. A thermal camera shows that warm bloom through tile, vinyl and even thin carpet, often spreading outward from the leak point in a recognizable shape. It only works on hot lines. We use it to narrow the search before the acoustic check.

    Acoustic Slab Leak Detection in Fort Mill

    Water escaping a pressurized pipe makes a hiss that carries up through concrete. Our ground microphones hear it. We move the pickup across the floor in a grid over the traced pipe route, finding where the sound is loudest and confirming it from another position, which works best on copper and less well on PEX.

    What Our Repipe Crews Do in Rock Hill, SC

    Galvanized Pipe Replacement for Older Homes in Rock Hill, SC

    Older homes may still have galvanized steel pipe that rusts from the inside out. Low pressure, brown water and leaks at threaded joints are the typical signs. Old galvanized gets removed or abandoned and replaced with PEX or copper, and most homes see better pressure and clearer water right away.

    PEX Repiping in Rock Hill, SC

    PEX is flexible plastic tubing that can run long distances without a fitting, which means fewer joints hidden in your walls. Fewer joints, fewer leaks. PEX shrugs off scale and chlorine, survives freezing better than copper and installs faster, so it is our most common repipe material. We use PEX-A with cold expansion fittings that keep the full inside diameter of the pipe open. That keeps pressure up at the far fixtures.

    Urgent Repipes After Repeated Failures in Rock Hill, SC

    A widespread pipe failure sometimes forces a repipe sooner than you planned. We make the house safe and usable first, with temporary repairs that get you water, then schedule the full repipe as soon as the crew and permit allow. We move fast. Even urgent repipes usually take just two to four days after we begin.

    Type L Copper Repiping in Rock Hill

    Copper has been plumbing homes for generations, and done right it lasts a long time. Our copper repipes use Type L with proper hangers and isolators so the pipe does not wear against framing. All joints use lead-free solder. Copper can pinhole in aggressive water, so we ask about your water and any past leaks before recommending it over PEX.

    Residential Repiping Plans in Rock Hill

    Every house is piped a little differently, so a repipe starts with a walk-through. Our estimator counts fixtures, checks access, finds the main and asks about any planned remodels before choosing a route. Then we size the lines. A three-bath house with a tankless heater may need 1-inch main trunk lines to keep showers steady when the washer kicks on.

    Repipe Costs Versus Leak Repairs in Rock Hill, SC

    A whole-home PEX repipe for an average house often runs $5,000 to $12,000, with copper costing more. One slab leak with access and repair often costs $1,000 to $4,000. Do the math. After two or three leaks, a repipe often pays for itself, and we give you a side-by-side comparison so you can make that call with real numbers in front of you.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Repipe Specialists in Rock Hill

    Most whole-home repipes in Rock Hill, SC fall somewhere between $4,000 and $15,000. Bigger homes cost more. Square footage, fixture count, material choice and the amount of drywall work along the route set the final price. We quote it in writing.

    Most whole-home repipes take three to five days, including patching. Large houses may need more time. We turn the water off in the morning and back on before we leave each evening, so you can cook, shower and do dishes at night even while the job is still in progress.

    We commonly use Uponor PEX-A with ProPEX fittings and Type L copper from domestic mills. We also use Viega ProPress fittings for copper work.

    Even with softer supply water in Rock Hill, a yearly water heater flush still helps clear the sediment that settles in the bottom of the tank. Hard water deposits calcium and magnesium scale inside pipe over time. Pressure falls as scale builds. In metal pipe, scale also speeds up corrosion, so repiping with PEX-A and adding a softener or filter during the job can protect the new plumbing and your fixtures for years.

    PEX resists scale and pinhole corrosion and handles expansion well, which makes it a strong choice for hard water. Copper lasts but is pricier and can pinhole in corrosive water.

    A repipe needs a permit, specialized tools, code knowledge and a passing pressure test. It takes skill and time. Hidden leaks, failed inspections or a voided pipe warranty from a DIY job can easily cost more than a licensed crew would have.

    Copper is rigid metal pipe with a long track record. PEX bends instead. PEX is cheaper, quicker and quieter and handles freezes better, while copper tolerates sun and heat and suits owners who want metal, so it depends on your house.

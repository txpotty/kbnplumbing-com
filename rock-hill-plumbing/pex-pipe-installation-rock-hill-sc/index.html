    Copper to PEX Conversions in Rock Hill

    Pinhole leaks in copper rarely show up alone. Aggressive water, fast flow and soil contact under slabs can pit copper from the inside, and the first few pinholes are typically followed by more. Private wells are common outside town limits, and some South Carolina well water carries iron, low pH or sediment that can stain fixtures and wear down water heaters. Converting to PEX stops the cycle. We use listed transition fittings anywhere PEX ties into copper that stays.

    Full Home PEX Repiping in Rock Hill

    Galvanized steel rusts from the inside out. Give it four or five decades and the inside is so packed with rust that a half-inch pipe barely flows, and the first draw in the morning runs brown. A full PEX repipe replaces all of it. You get real pressure back, and the discolored water goes away with the old pipe.

    Leak Repair with PEX in Rock Hill, SC

    A single bad section is not always a reason to repipe. Once you are on the third patch, we will tell you straight whether the rest of the copper is heading the same way.

    Remodel and Addition PEX Rough-In in Rock Hill

    Rough-in heights matter more than people think. A shower valve set two inches too low or a vanity stub that misses the cabinet back means opening the wall again after the tile is up, which is expensive and slow.     PEX-B Crimp and Clamp Systems in Rock Hill

    PEX-B is stiffer, costs less per foot, and joins with copper crimp rings or stainless cinch clamps over brass or poly-alloy fittings. Since a crimp is only as good as the tool that made it, every ring gets a go/no-go gauge check, and any ring that fails gets cut off and redone. That takes seconds. Skipping it is how leaks start.

    PEX for Remodels & Additions in Rock Hill, SC

    Opening a wall for a kitchen remodel is the cheapest time to replace old supply lines. Additions get their own sizing. A far-end bathroom may need a three-quarter inch feed to hold pressure.

    PEX Main Water Line Installation in Rock Hill

    The main from the meter to the house is the one line everything else depends on.

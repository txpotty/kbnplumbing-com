    Kitchen Line Rooter Service in Columbia, SC

    Kitchen lines clog slowly, over months, with grease, soap and food scraps that cool and harden on the pipe wall. We go in through the trap or a cleanout with a medium cable and spiral cutter. Thick grease calls for a follow-up jet. The jet washes the walls clean, which keeps the line open much longer than the cable alone.

    Rooter Plus Hydro Jetting in Columbia

    A cable cuts roots, and a jetter washes out what is left. The pair clears dense roots and old grease better than either tool alone. The pipe gets filmed before we jet. This combination is common for restaurants and for homes where the same line plugs every year, usually from roots at one bad joint.

    Sewer Root Removal in Columbia

    Roots cause more main sewer clogs than anything else on older streets. They enter through tiny gaps in clay and cast iron joints and grow into thick mats that catch everything flowing past. We cut them back with a root saw on a heavy cable. Most of these lines need cutting every year or two.

    Toilet and Bathroom Rooter Service in Columbia

    Toilets clog from too much paper, wipes and toys. First comes a closet auger with a sleeve that keeps it from scratching the bowl. If that does not reach it, we pull the toilet and root the line below, then reset it on a new wax ring. Bathroom sinks take a lighter cable.

    Camera Inspection for Rooter Services in Columbia, SC

    On a hard or repeat clog, KBN Plumbing sends a camera down after the cable. With a sonde and locator we can mark the exact spot and depth on your lawn. That turns a guess into a measurement. If the pipe is broken, you know exactly where, so any repair in Columbia, SC is one small dig rather than a trench across the yard.

    Hydro-Jetting Services in Columbia, SC

    Jetting puts water through a nozzle at 3,000 to 4,000 psi and scrubs the pipe wall clean. Grease, scale and fine roots that a cable rides over come off with the water. Private wells are common outside town limits, and some South Carolina well water carries iron, low pH or sediment that can stain fixtures and wear down water heaters. We camera the line first. Pressure that high can damage cracked clay or thin cast iron, so we jet only after we have looked at the pipe.

    Rooting vs. Pipe Repair in Columbia, SC

    Rooting makes sense when the pipe is sound and just clogged. When roots return in months, or the cable hangs on a broken joint every time, repairing that section starts to save money. A camera answers the question. Rooting usually runs a few hundred dollars, and lining or replacing a section costs more but ends the repeat calls.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Rooter Services in Columbia

    Rooting a sink, tub or toilet usually costs $125 to $300. Main sewer rooting through a cleanout usually runs $200 to $500. With hydro jetting added, expect roughly $450 to $1,000, depending on how long the line is and how heavy the grease is. After-hours calls cost more.

    Yes, most rooter jobs come with a limited guarantee. The terms depend on the line and what we found, and they are written on your estimate. Roots are the exception. A line with broken pipe or heavy root intrusion may carry a shorter guarantee, since roots will regrow until the pipe itself is fixed.

    Clay or older cast iron lines with trees nearby usually need rooting every 12 to 24 months. Newer PVC lines may go years without it. A camera helps set the schedule. How quickly roots return after the last cut tells us if yearly or every-other-year service fits your line.

    Chemicals sometimes open a small hole in a hair clog, but they will not touch roots or a grease plug. They can soften old PVC glue joints and pit cast iron over time. They also burn skin. A rooter cable removes the clog itself rather than eating a channel through it, so the line stays open far longer.

    Keep grease, coffee grounds and wipes out of drains. Hair strainers in tubs and showers help. Run hot water after using the disposal, and scrape plates into the trash before rinsing so less food goes down the kitchen line. Root-prone lines need cutting every year or two.

    Plan on 1 to 3 hours for a single clogged drain. A root-choked main or a long jetting job may take 3 to 6 hours. You get a time estimate first. Once the tech sees the cleanout and asks which fixtures are slow, they can give you a closer estimate of how long it will run.

    Chemical drain openers rarely clear a real clog and can eat at old pipe. Old cast iron and the thin brass traps found in Columbia's older homes take the most damage. They also make our job riskier. Let us know if you used a chemical, because that water splashes when the trap comes off.

    Routine drain cleaning in Columbia, SC does not usually need a permit. Digging up the yard, replacing the sewer or making a major repair usually requires one. We handle that paperwork. If the rooter work turns up a problem that needs a permit, we tell you before any repair gets scheduled.

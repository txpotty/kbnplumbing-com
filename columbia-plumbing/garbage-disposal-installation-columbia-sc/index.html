    Disposal Installation in Columbia, SC: The Details

    Disposal Rough-In for New Construction in Columbia

    At rough-in we set the drain height and the outlet location before the cabinets go in. For a kitchen with a disposal, we usually set the drain stub lower than a plain sink would need, so the trap can sit below the disposal outlet with room for proper fall. A switched outlet goes in at this stage. We mark its spot so it clears the trap.

    Commercial Disposal Installation in Columbia

    Commercial disposals run 1 to 5 horsepower and typically come with a control panel and solenoid valve and need their own circuit. We set the unit on the mounting collar, run the drain to the grease trap where the local rules require it, and wire the controls so the water runs whenever the motor does. Health codes vary. We check the requirement before we order anything.

    Dishwasher Connection With Disposal Installation in Columbia

    Every new disposal has a plug sealing the dishwasher inlet. We knock it out with a screwdriver and hammer, fish the plug out of the chamber and connect the hose with a new clamp. A plug left in backs up the dishwasher. We also install an air gap or high loop, so dirty sink water cannot flow back into the dishwasher while the disposal is draining.

    Batch-Feed Disposal Installation in Columbia, SC

    A batch-feed disposal will not start until the lid is in, and the lid holds a magnet that closes a switch inside the unit. No wall switch is needed, which makes them handy in kitchens where running a new switch would mean opening finished walls. They are slower to use. Families with small kids often like the trade.

    Replacing an Old Disposal in Columbia, SC

    The old unit usually drops free with a turn of the mount ring, once the power is off and the dishwasher hose and discharge tube are disconnected. If the new unit is the same brand and mount style, the swap is faster, but we still replace the flange gasket and putty when the old seal has hardened. Seals harden with age. The old disposal leaves with us.

    Standard Disposal Installation in Columbia

    Most households in Columbia do well with a continuous-feed unit between 1/3 and 1/2 horsepower. We remove the old unit if there is one, set a new flange in fresh plumber's putty, hang the disposal on a new mount and connect it to the trap with a new discharge tube and gasket. Then we wire it and test it. We listen for a smooth spin and watch the drain clear.

    High-Horsepower Disposal Installation in Columbia

    A 3/4 or 1 HP unit takes up more room and weighs more than a basic unit, so we measure the cabinet first. Taller units can push the discharge outlet below the wall stub, and when that happens we either lower the drain at the wall or pick a model with a higher outlet so water does not sit in the chamber. We also check the circuit. Bigger motors draw more at startup.

    First-Time Disposal Installation in Columbia

    For kitchens that have never had a disposal, we start by checking the sink flange, drain height and electrical supply. The mount goes on, the unit gets hung and the discharge tube is connected to a new trap, with the wall stub lowered if it sits too high. An electrician adds power if needed.

    Slab Leak Warning Signs Every South Carolina Homeowner Should Know

    By Aaron Mitchell · October 15, 2026 · 5 min read

    What a Slab Leak Is

    A slab leak is a break in a water or drain line that runs under or through the concrete floor of your house. Nobody sees it at first. Most supply lines under a slab are soft copper or PEX, and the first failure is usually a pinhole about the size of a pencil point. Years of shifting soil and seasonal temperature change keep stressing that buried line.

    Warm weather for most of the year means water heaters, irrigation lines and outdoor fixtures in South Carolina see steady use with little seasonal rest.

    Concrete hides the damage, so a leak can run for months before anyone notices. By then the water has usually soaked the pad under the flooring. We have pulled up laminate where the slab underneath was wet from one wall to the other, and the only clue the owner had was a water bill that doubled. Early means a smaller hole in the floor.

    Clay that swells when wet and shrinks in dry spells can stress buried sewer lines over time, leading to sags, separated joints and root intrusion.

    Soil under a house expands when wet and shrinks when dry, and every cycle puts a little more load on pipe that is locked in concrete. Copper thins out wherever it rubs on gravel or a piece of rebar. Hard water speeds that wear from the inside. PVC drain lines under the slab crack at the joints more often than along the pipe.

    Signs You Can Check Yourself

    A Water Bill That Jumps for No Reason

    Pull up the last six months of bills and compare the gallons, not the dollars. A rise of 20 or 30 percent with no change in how the house uses water is worth checking. Then do the meter test. Shut every fixture off, write down the meter reading, and wait two hours without running anything. A meter that moved means water is leaving the system somewhere between the meter and your fixtures, and under the slab is a common spot.

    Warm Spots on the Floor

    A hot water line leaking under tile will heat the slab above it. Walk the house barefoot in the morning. What you want to find is one warm patch while the rest of the tile is cool, usually close to a bathroom or the kitchen. Pets find these spots before people do, so watch where the dog likes to lie in winter.

    Running Water With Everything Off

    Stand in a quiet house at night and listen near the floor. A hiss or a faint rush with no fixture running points to pressure escaping from a supply line. Some leaks are loud enough to hear. Others need an acoustic listening device, which is what our techs use to pinpoint the spot within a foot or two.

    New Cracks in Walls or Flooring

    Water under a slab washes out fine soil and lets the concrete settle unevenly. The usual clue is a crack in the tile or concrete running straight across a room. Doors that suddenly stick are another clue. A drywall crack angling up from the corner of a door frame means the slab shifted, and a leak is a common cause.

    Musty Smell or Mildew at the Baseboards

    Carpet over a leak stays damp and starts to smell like a basement. Check the baseboards and tack strip for dark stains. Mildew low on a wall with no fixture behind it usually means the moisture is coming up from the slab. A moisture meter confirms it in seconds.

    Getting a Slab Leak Found and Fixed

    Do not start breaking concrete on a guess. Our techs pressure test each line first, then use acoustic and thermal gear to mark the exact location. From there the choice is usually between a spot repair through the slab and a reroute through the attic or walls. The right choice depends mostly on pipe age and how many leaks the house has already had.

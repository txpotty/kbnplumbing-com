    Frequently Asked Questions About Copper Pipe Leak Repair in Greer

    A minor, accessible pinhole repair starts around $200. Hidden leaks cost more. Complex repairs like under-slab leaks can run past $2,000, with location, severity and footage of new pipe setting most of the price. You get a written number after locating.

    Most accessible repairs take one to two hours. Hidden leaks run longer. Leaks inside walls or ceilings can take two to four hours, while slab repairs can take most of a day. We give you the timeline first.

    Every repair is warrantied. We write the warranty down. Our workmanship warranty covers the repair, while the parts carry separate manufacturer coverage. You see the terms first.

    The pipe wall wears through from the inside. Water chemistry drives it. Aggressive water, high velocity, excess pressure, poor original soldering and stray electrical current from grounding issues all erode the copper over time until a tiny hole opens. Hot lines get it worst.

    Clamps and epoxy putty can stop a drip for a day. Temporary fixes fail. A proper repair means cutting out the thin copper and soldering or pressing in new pipe, and a DIY patch that lets go while you are at work can flood a room in the time it takes to drive home. Treat it as a stopgap.

    Insurance often covers sudden water damage. Not the pipe itself. The cost of repairing old or corroded copper is usually yours, but the resulting damage to the house may be claimable. Check with your agent. We provide documentation.

    All standard copper used in homes and businesses. Type M, L and K. Our repairs use copper and fittings from established manufacturers that meet ASTM and NSF standards, with every part listed for drinking water. No off-brand fittings.

    A repair clamp or epoxy can buy time in an emergency. It will not last. A plumber should cut out the damaged section and install proper fittings, especially inside walls where a failed patch can rot framing before you notice. Shut the water off first.

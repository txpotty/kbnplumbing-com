    Frequently Asked Questions About Drain Repairs in Greer

    Trap and slip-joint repairs typically cost $150 to $350. Pipe sections run higher. A section of drain pipe usually runs $400 to $1,200, and cast iron stack replacement can range from $2,000 to $6,000 with drywall, flooring and access driving most of the spread. You get the price in writing first.

    Hydro-jetting scours the pipe with water at several thousand psi. It cleans wall to wall. A snake only punches a hole through a clog, while a jetter removes grease, scale and fine roots from the full circumference of the pipe. We camera first on old or brittle pipe.

    Small fixture drain repairs usually do not. Stack work typically does. Replacing drain stacks, relocating drains or adding new lines often needs a permit, depending on the city and how much of the system is being changed. The permit is on us if needed.

    A trap swap or a fixture drain fix is usually an hour or two. Main lines take longer. Replacing a section of main line can take a full day or several days, with permits and digging as the main things that stretch it out. The schedule is in the quote.

    Cleaning removes a blockage from a sound pipe. Repair changes the pipe itself. A crack, a sag, a broken fitting or a missing vent will keep causing clogs no matter how often the line gets cleaned, which is why recurring clogs usually call for a fix instead of another cleaning. We check on video.

    Most trap and fixture drain repairs take one to two hours. Wall work takes longer. Replacing drain pipe inside a wall or under a floor can take half a day to a full day, depending on how much finish material has to come out. We give you a time with the quote.

    A dry trap is the most common cause. Unused fixtures dry out. A cracked drain fitting, a failed wax ring under the toilet or a blocked vent all leak gas too, and a smoke test usually finds the source in minutes. The fix is often quick.

    Snugging a slip nut or replacing a plastic trap is a fair DIY job. Do not crank it down. If the leak comes from a cracked tailpiece, a corroded metal drain or somewhere inside the wall, it needs a plumber who can fix the pipe and look for rot nearby. Water damage under sinks spreads quietly.

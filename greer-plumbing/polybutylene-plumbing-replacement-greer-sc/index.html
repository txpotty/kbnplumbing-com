    How We Replace Polybutylene in Greer, SC

    Full Home Repiping with PEX in Greer

    Most poly replacements we do use PEX-A, a flexible tubing that runs long distances with few fittings. For slab houses, new PEX-A runs through the attic and drops down interior walls, so none of it sits under the floor. It also tolerates freezing better. We use expansion fittings that keep the full inside diameter open, so pressure at the far shower stays strong.

    Emergency Polybutylene Burst Repair in Greer

    Poly fittings tend to let go during sharp temperature swings, when the pipe expands and contracts more than usual. Before a forecast freeze, disconnecting garden hoses, covering hose bibs and letting a faucet drip usually does the job to protect a home. Close the main if a line bursts, and call us to stop it, patch it and get water back on. Then we plan the full fix. Waiting for the next burst is the most expensive way to deal with polybutylene.

    Full Home Polybutylene Repiping in Greer

    A full replacement takes every poly supply line out of service, not just the sections that have failed. Partial jobs leave risk behind. New pipe goes from the main to every fixture and appliance, each one gets a new quarter-turn stop and the system is tested before any wall is closed.

    Polybutylene Leak Repair in Greer

    Repairs on polybutylene are temporary by nature, because the pipe next to the repair is the same age. A repair buys time. We cut out the failed piece and splice in PEX with listed fittings, stopping the leak and giving you time to plan the full job.

    Fitting Failure and Emergency Repair in Greer

    Acetal plastic and copper crimp ring fittings are often the first part of a poly system to fail. A burst fitting in an attic or wall can cause major damage in minutes. We respond to emergency leaks, make temporary repairs and then schedule a full replacement, because stopping the problem for good means taking the poly system out of service entirely.

    Manufactured Home Poly Replacement in Greer, SC

    Manufactured homes from the poly era often have polybutylene lines in the floor or the belly underneath. We replace those lines with PEX sized for the home and secure them properly to the frame. Exposed lines get checked. Before a forecast freeze, disconnecting garden hoses, covering hose bibs and letting a faucet drip usually does the job to protect a home. We inspect heat tape and insulation on any exposed pipe and replace what is worn, so the new lines stay protected.

    Copper Replacement for Polybutylene in Greer, SC

    Some homeowners in Greer, SC want copper when they take out polybutylene. That works fine. Our copper replacements use thicker Type L pipe with lead-free soldered or pressed joints, not the thinner Type M. Expect a higher price and a slightly longer job with copper, in exchange for good heat and UV tolerance and a long history.

    Polybutylene Identification and Inspection in Greer

    Polybutylene is usually gray, though it can be white, blue or black, and is often stamped PB2110. Check the heater first. It also shows up under sinks, at the main and through garage walls, and we follow it into the attic to see how much of the house it feeds.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Polybutylene Plumbing Replacement in Greer

    Most PEX replacements of polybutylene cost $5,000 to $12,000 for an average home. Copper costs more, and yard service lines often add $1,500 to $4,000.

    Both work well in South Carolina homes. PEX is flexible, handles freezing better and usually costs less to install. Copper is rigid, lasts a long time and tolerates sunlight and heat, and we will help you choose based on your home, your water and your budget.

    Most of our PEX replacements use Uponor AquaPEX with ProPEX expansion fittings. We also use Viega PEX. Copper jobs use domestic Type L, and we keep one PEX brand throughout each house so the warranty holds.

    Every poly replacement we do comes with a written warranty on materials and labor. Manufacturer coverage adds more. You get both warranties in writing at the end of the job, along with photos and the permit record, so everything is in one folder if you ever need it.

    Whole-home repiping usually requires a permit and inspection. Our office files the permit and books the inspection.

    For PEX, we use trusted brands like Uponor, Viega and Zurn. Uponor is our usual pick. Copper replacements use Type L instead of thin Type M, since the heavier wall lasts longer.

    Private wells are common outside town limits, and some South Carolina well water carries iron, low pH or sediment that can stain fixtures and wear down water heaters. Hard water does not cause poly to fail the way chlorine does. Scale builds inside. Mineral scale cuts flow and adds strain to brittle pipe, which is why a softener loop during the repipe can make sense in hard water.

    Both are good choices. PEX usually costs less. PEX bends, handles freezes and movement and goes in quickly, and copper is stiff, heat tolerant and long proven, so it comes down to budget, water and preference.

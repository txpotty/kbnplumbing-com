    Frequently Asked Questions About Well Pump Replacement in Sumter

    Plan on $1,500 to $3,500 for a typical residential job. A deeper well or a bigger pump pushes it higher, and a new pressure tank or wire run adds to it, sometimes well past that range. The quote follows the diagnosis.

    Four to eight hours for most jobs. A deep set or a well head that is hard to reach can push the job to a full day. Water is usually back on the same day.

    Eight to fifteen years is common. Sediment and frequent cycling shorten that.

    Shallow well pumps, usually jet pumps, sit above ground and pull water by suction from about 25 feet or less. Submersibles go down in the water and push it to the surface. Suction cannot lift much past 25 feet.

    We do not recommend it. You are dealing with 230 volts and hundreds of pounds of wet pipe, plus the risk of dropping the pump into the well. Retrieving a dropped pump costs more than the whole job.

    If the tank is old or waterlogged, replace it with the pump. Short cycling from a dead tank is one of the top reasons a submersible motor dies young.

    Location is the difference. A jet pump sits above ground and pulls water up by suction, which limits it to shallow wells, while a submersible hangs in the water and pushes it up from hundreds of feet. Submersibles are more efficient at depth.

    Pressure Switch Replacement in Sumter

    The pressure switch tells the pump when to start and stop. Pitted contacts, clogged sensing tubes and insect damage are common causes of failure. We replace the switch, clean or replace the tube and set the cut-in and cut-out pressures to match your tank. This is one of the most affordable well repairs.

    Submersible Well Pump Repair in Sumter

    Submersible pumps are common in residential wells, designed to operate submerged within the well casing. When these pumps fail, symptoms often include no water, low water pressure, or strange noises from the well. Our technicians are skilled at diagnosing issues like motor burnout, damaged impellers, or faulty check valves that can affect submersible pump performance. We use specialized equipment for safe and efficient pump extraction and reinstallation, protecting the integrity of your well system.

    Well Pump Pressure Tank Issues in Sumter

    The pressure tank works together with your well pump to maintain consistent water pressure and reduce pump cycling. A waterlogged tank, a ruptured diaphragm, or a faulty air valve can cause rapid pump cycling, fluctuating water pressure, or even no water at all. Our team carefully diagnoses pressure tank problems and performs necessary repairs or replacements. We ensure your tank is properly sized and charged for optimal system performance in Sumter.

    Control Box and Electrical Repair in Sumter, SC

    Three-wire submersible pumps rely on a control box with a start capacitor and relay. Hurricanes and tropical storms bring heavy rain to South Carolina, and flooded streets can push water back up through floor drains and low sewer connections. Lightning and power surges often damage these components. We test capacitors, relays and overload protection and replace failed parts. A surge protector at the control box can help reduce repeat failures.

    Well Pump Repair vs Replacement Cost in Sumter, SC

    Control and pressure switch repairs often cost $150 to $500, while a pressure tank replacement typically runs $600 to $1,500. Pulling and replacing a submersible pump can range from $1,800 to $5,000 or more, depending on depth, horsepower and wire. If your pump is older and already needs pulling, replacing it at the same time can save a second pull later. We lay out both options with honest numbers.

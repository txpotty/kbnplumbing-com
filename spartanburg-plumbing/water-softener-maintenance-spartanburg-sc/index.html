    Hardness Testing at the Tap in Spartanburg

    We test with a titration kit, counting drops until the sample changes color, because test strips are too rough for setting a valve. We take the raw sample from an unsoftened hose bib or with the softener on bypass. Softened water comes from the kitchen tap.

    Control Valve and Injector Cleaning in Spartanburg, SC

    Brine draw depends on a venturi injector, a tiny nozzle and throat that pulls brine out of the salt tank as water rushes past. A few specks of grit can stop it cold. We pull the injector and screen, clean them, and replace the seals and spacers on valves like the Fleck 5600 and Clack WS1 when they show wear.

    Brine Tank Cleanout and Inspection in Spartanburg, SC

    Brine tanks collect a gray sludge of salt fines and insoluble grit, and it builds up faster with cheap rock salt. The tank gets emptied, scrubbed and rinsed. Then we look for cracks near the overflow elbow and the brine well and refill with fresh salt.

    Bypass Valve and Drain Line Repairs in Spartanburg, SC

    A bypass valve that does not seat fully lets raw water blend into the softened supply, and the hardness test is usually the first clue. The bypass gets rebuilt or swapped out and check the drain line for kinks. Code requires an air gap at the drain.

    Commercial Softener Service in Spartanburg

    Commercial units work harder. Restaurants and laundromats can burn through a softener's capacity in a single day, so twin-tank systems swap tanks, keeping one in service while the other regenerates. We set a service schedule around your hours so the water never goes hard during a rush.

    Salt-Free Conditioner Service in Spartanburg

    Salt-free conditioners, usually template assisted crystallization units, do not remove hardness. A hardness test still reads high afterward. They change the minerals into tiny crystals that are less likely to stick. The media needs replacing every three to five years, and the sediment prefilter ahead of it needs changing every few months, which is the part most owners skip.

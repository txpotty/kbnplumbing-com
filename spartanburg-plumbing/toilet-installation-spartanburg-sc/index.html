    Frequently Asked Questions About Toilet Installation in Spartanburg

    Setting a customer-supplied standard toilet usually costs $200 to $400 in labor. Add a mid-range toilet and the total is usually $450 to $900. A wall-hung toilet with its carrier runs $1,500 to $3,500 or more, since the wall must be opened and refinished. We quote a flat price.

    Both seal fine on a flange set at the right height. Wax is cheap and proven. Foam or rubber wax-free seals forgive a toilet that has to be lifted and reset, and they do not soften the way wax can in a warm bathroom or over radiant floor heat. We pick based on your flange height.

    It is a toilet with the seat about 17 to 19 inches off the floor, roughly chair height, compared with 15 inches or so on a standard bowl. Knees and hips usually like it. Kids and shorter adults sometimes find their feet do not rest flat, so in a shared bathroom we sometimes suggest a standard bowl with a grab bar instead.

    Most municipal water in South Carolina comes from rivers and reservoirs, so it tends to run fairly soft and leaves less scale than in many other states. Hard water leaves scale in the rim jets and on the fill valve over time. Weak flushes follow. A toilet with a well-glazed trapway and an easy-to-reach fill valve holds up better, and a cup of vinegar left in the overflow tube overnight now and then keeps the rim jets open.

    It depends on the floor and the flange. A straight swap on a solid flange is a reasonable DIY job. Hire out new tile jobs, broken flanges, wall-hung carriers and relocations, since mistakes there hide under the floor.

    Homeowners set basic toilets all the time. Flange height and bolt torque are where it goes wrong. A flange that sits below the finished floor needs an extender or a thicker seal, and a nut tightened one turn too far can crack the base, so on new floors and wall-hung toilets a pro install is the safer bet.

    TOTO and American Standard do well in independent flush tests, and Kohler is close behind, with parts at most supply houses. Gerber and Mansfield are solid on a budget. The right pick depends on your rough-in and who uses the room.

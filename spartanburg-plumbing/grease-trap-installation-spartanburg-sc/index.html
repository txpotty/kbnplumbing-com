    Hydro-Mechanical Grease Trap Installation in Spartanburg, SC

    Hydromechanical traps use baffles and a vented flow control to pull grease out of water moving at a set rate, usually 20 to 100 gallons per minute. You can put one under a sink, beside a dish station or in a pit in the slab. The flow control is not optional. Skip it and a full sink drain overruns the rating, which sends grease right past the baffles. We look for units certified to PDI G-101 or ASME A112.14.3.

    Gravity Grease Trap Installation in Spartanburg

    A gravity interceptor works on time, not machinery. Wastewater slows down in the tank long enough for grease to float and solids to settle. We size by fixture count and retention time, then match the tank to what the local pretreatment office for your building will accept. No moving parts to fail.

    Large Outdoor Grease Interceptor Installation in Spartanburg

    Outdoor gravity interceptors run from 750 gallons to well past 2,000, and setting one means an excavator on site and a traffic-rated lid if it sits in the drive lane. Before anything ships, we check that the tank and covers carry an H-20 traffic rating where trucks will roll over them. Most go in over two to four days. The inlet invert has to sit a couple of inches above the outlet, and we shoot both with a laser level before backfill.

    Restaurant Grease Trap Compliance in Spartanburg, SC

    Most sewer authorities run a FOG program with an inspector who opens your trap and checks your pump-out manifests against the schedule they set. Fail that and you can face fines or a surcharge on your sewer bill. We install to pass. That means a sample port where the program wants one and a unit sized to the number their formula gives, not the number the last contractor guessed.

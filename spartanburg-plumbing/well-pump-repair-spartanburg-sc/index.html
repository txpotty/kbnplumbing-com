    Well Pump Problems We Fix in Spartanburg, SC

    Pressure Switch Replacement in Spartanburg

    The pressure switch tells the pump when to start and stop. Pitted contacts, a clogged sensing tube and ant nests are the usual reasons one fails. We replace the switch, clean or replace the tube and set the cut-in and cut-out pressures to match your tank, typically 40 and 60 psi. One of the cheaper well repairs.

    Pressure Tank Repair in Spartanburg, SC

    A waterlogged pressure tank makes the pump short cycle, turning on and off every few seconds. Fast cycling kills pumps young. We check the air charge, inspect the bladder and recharge or replace the tank, then confirm the pump runs at least a minute per cycle. Steadier pressure follows.

    Well Pump Diagnostic Services in Spartanburg, SC

    Voltage, amperage, pressure and flow rate tell us most of what is happening down the well without pulling a single length of pipe. A weak motor draws high amps. A clogged intake or a dropping water level shows up as low flow with normal amps, and that changes the repair entirely. Testing first saves a pull.

    Jet Pump Repair in Spartanburg, SC

    Shallow wells and booster systems often use jet pumps. Common problems are loss of prime, a leaking shaft seal and a worn impeller. Our crew in Spartanburg restores prime, replaces worn parts and checks the suction line for leaks that let air in. That matters most where water levels rise and fall through the year, since a dropping level can starve the pump and cost it prime.

    Deep Well Pump Repair in Spartanburg

    Deep wells get submersible pumps. They push water up, run quietly and last, but motors, impellers and splices still wear out. After pulling the pump, we test each part and replace only the failed one. Heat-shrink splice kits and a torque arrestor protect the new work in your Spartanburg, SC well.

    Grundfos Well Pump Repair in Spartanburg

    Grundfos SQ and SQE pumps have built-in electronics that protect the motor and report faults. Most Grundfos calls involve a sensor or a drive fault. KBN Plumbing reads the fault code, checks the wiring and tests the pressure sensor before replacing anything in a Spartanburg, SC well. Usually the electronics are fine.

    Well Pump Electrical Troubleshooting in Spartanburg, SC

    Electrical faults cause a big share of well calls, from a tripped breaker to a burned contact in the pressure switch. The control box capacitor and relay are next on the list. We lock out the power before testing anything. All wiring and splices meet South Carolina electrical code before the pump restarts.

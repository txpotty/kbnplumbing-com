    Sump Pump Types We Install in Spartanburg, SC

    High-Capacity Sump Pump Upgrades in Spartanburg

    If your pump runs every 30 seconds during a storm and still loses ground, it is undersized for the water coming in. First we time the pump cycles and measure the pit refill rate. From there it is math. A half-horse or three-quarter-horse pump with a higher gallons-per-minute rating at your lift height may be the answer, sometimes paired with a 2-inch discharge.

    Sewage Ejector Pump Replacement in Spartanburg

    Basement bathrooms below the sewer line drain into a sealed, vented basin, and the ejector pump in that basin has to pass 2-inch solids. That is not a sump pump job. The ejector and check valve get replaced, then we reseal the lid and check the vent connection. Leaky lids let sewer gas into the basement.

    Float Switch and Check Valve Replacement in Spartanburg

    Stuck floats and leaking check valves cause more sump failures than dead motors do. A check valve that leaks lets the discharge column fall back into the pit, so the pump runs again for the same water and wears itself out early. Every pump we install gets a new check valve. Vertical switches suit tight pits, and tethered floats suit wide ones.

    Sizing a Submersible Sump Pump in Spartanburg

    Horsepower matters less than people think. A third-horse pump moves plenty of water for most homes, as long as the vertical lift to the discharge is under about 15 feet. Before we choose, we read the pump curve at your real discharge height. A pump that is too big empties the pit too fast and wears itself out.

    Zoeller Sump Pump Replacement in Spartanburg, SC

    Zoeller cast iron pumps, the M53 and M63 in particular, are what we pull off the truck most often because they take heat and hard cycling better than plastic housings. An existing Zoeller can usually be swapped for a current model with the same 1.5-inch outlet and float style. Need more lift? The M98 steps up to half horsepower.

    Elevator and Garage Pit Pumps in Spartanburg

    Elevator pits and parking garage sumps often require oil-sensing controls that stop the pump from sending hydraulic fluid or motor oil into the storm drain. Pumps and oil-sensing panels get replaced to code. Duplex setups with alternating pumps give you a spare if one quits. Insurers sometimes ask for proof.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Sump Pump Replacement in Spartanburg

    A standard swap usually runs $500 to $1,200 installed. Add $800 to $1,800 for a battery backup system. Sewage ejector pumps are a bigger job, generally $1,200 to $2,500, because the basin has to be sealed and tied into the plumbing vent system.

    The primary pump runs on household current and does the daily work. The backup, battery or water powered, sits a few inches higher and only runs if the primary fails, loses power or cannot keep up with a heavy inflow. Most basements are better off with one.

    If storms knock out power in your area or you have finished space below grade, yes. A battery unit with a high-water alarm usually gives six to ten hours of intermittent pumping on a full charge.

    Budget a morning or an afternoon. The work includes pulling the old pump, cleaning the basin, installing the new pump and check valve, and running a fill test. Longer if the basin or discharge needs work.

    A pump that hums without moving water, rattles, runs nonstop or shows rust on the housing is near the end. Age matters too. Past 7 to 10 years, most pumps are living on borrowed time, even if they still start when you pour a bucket into the pit.

    Like-for-like pump swaps usually do not need a permit in most jurisdictions. Adding a circuit or moving the discharge line can require one, and every city handles it a little differently. We check the local rule before we start.

    Mostly cast iron Zoeller and Liberty units for primary pumps, with Wayne and Little Giant for some backup and utility jobs. Other brands are fine if they fit the pit. Plastic housings are cheaper. They also run hotter and die younger.

    Manufacturer coverage on the pump usually runs 1 to 3 years for plastic models and up to 5 for cast iron. Our labor warranty sits on top of that. You will see the terms in writing.
